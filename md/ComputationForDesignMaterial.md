Detailed Briefing Document: Understanding Computational Design

It explores the core definitions, key concepts, underlying principles, and the transformative role of this approach in contemporary design.

1. Defining Computational Design: Beyond Simple Computer Use
   Computational Design is not merely about using computers in design; it's a profound integration of computational thinking and automation into the design process. It is fundamentally defined as "designing by utilizing some form of calculation, specifically leveraging the power of computing through rules." This means that design is approached through a lens of systematic calculation and algorithmic logic.
   • Leveraging Computing Power: The bedrock of computational design is the harnessing of a computer's immense processing capabilities. This allows for the "maximization of automation for convergent tasks or optimization." This translates to achieving tasks that converge towards a specific goal, often in the most efficient or effective way possible.
   • The Meaning of 'Calculation': In this context, 'calculation' refers to the computer's ability to solve complex problems through binary (True/False) based arithmetic operations. Designers "compose" design data such as color (RGB) and form (points, lines, surfaces) and translate them into a format that computers can process to execute design activities.
   • Algorithms as Design Logic: A crucial aspect is the formalization of design methods into computable rules. The sources state that Computational Design involves "making design actions, such as handling points, lines, surfaces, and colors, understandable for a computer to calculate through algorithms, often referred to as design logic or methodologies." This highlights the importance of structuring a designer's thought processes and methodologies into algorithms that a computer can interpret and execute.
2. Problem Decomposition and Algorithmic Thinking
   A cornerstone principle of Computational Design is the ability to break down complex problems into smaller, manageable units, a concept central to computer science known as 'Problem Decomposition.' As stated, "When there are big problems, design problems that we need to solve, we break them down. We divide them into the smallest possible meaningful tasks."
   • Computer-Friendly Instructions: These decomposed, smaller problems are then translated into "a set of instructions through programming, which a computer can understand." This includes explicit commands like clicks, selections, or drawing actions within graphic tools, or more abstract code.
   • Formation of Algorithms: These "small pieces" or instructions, when assembled, form an "algorithm" for solving a problem. This algorithm can then evolve into a comprehensive design logic or methodology. For example, even a seemingly simple action like "drawing a circle" can be decomposed into smaller instructions: "select a center point," "select a second point," and "draw a circle connecting the center point and the second point."
3. The Evolving Role of the Designer and the Appeal of Computational Design
   Computational Design doesn't replace the designer's intuition or experience; rather, it amplifies and extends it by providing a framework to "articulate them more clearly as instructions."
   • Externalizing Designer Thought: Designers often implicitly calculate and make rapid judgments. Computational Design is the process of "describing" this intuitive thought process of the designer into explicit rules and procedures that a computer can understand and execute.
   • Enhancing Productivity and Unlocking Potential: The ultimate appeal of Computational Design lies in its capacity to empower designers with the power of computation. As the source highlights, it enables designers to "easily, diversely, quickly, or even to see potentials we might not have known, by utilizing computing power for calculations." This offers several key advantages:
   ◦ Efficiency: Automating repetitive and convergent tasks significantly accelerates the design process.
   ◦ Diversity: It allows for the rapid generation and exploration of a wide range of design alternatives.
   ◦ Optimization: Designs can be optimized to meet specific performance or aesthetic goals.
   ◦ Discovery of New Possibilities: It provides the potential to explore novel design forms and solutions that might be difficult or impossible to discover through human intuition alone.
4. Conclusion
   Computational Design is a transformative field that integrates computational thinking into the design process. By leveraging the immense processing power of computers, it facilitates the automation, optimization, and exploration of new possibilities in design. It acts as a powerful methodology that empowers designers to formalize their experience and intuition into algorithms and instructions, thereby enhancing both the efficiency and creativity of design problem-solving.

1. What is Parametric Design?
   Parametric design, at its core, involves defining design elements and their relationships through parameters. These parameters are essentially variables that control aspects of a design. The crucial element is the establishment of dependencies, relationships, and interactions between these parameters. This allows for a dynamic and adaptable design process where changes to one parameter can automatically propagate and affect others, leading to systematic design modifications or the generation of variations.

7. Summary
   In conclusion, Parametric Design is a design methodology that involves:
   • Defining parameters for design elements.

   ◦ Data in a computer is ultimately represented as numbers. "Data is ultimately a collection of numbers."
   ◦ This can be simplified to 0s and 1s, and even characters are represented numerically.
   ◦ Understanding the nature of data (its structure and representation) is crucial.

   ◦ An algorithm is essentially "a method of calculation." Since data is numbers, calculations are performed on them.
   ◦ Algorithms involve two primary steps: _ Repetition (Loops): Repeating a specific task. Examples include "for loops" and "while loops." _ Conditions (Conditionals): Making decisions based on certain criteria ("if this, then do that; if that, then do this"). This involves "if statements."
   ◦ Algorithms work by "continuously modifying the information (data) through conditions and repetition."

   • Low-Level: Refers to machine-centric ways of thinking and expression. It's more concrete and detailed, closer to how a computer understands instructions. Assembly language and C are given as examples, being closer to machine language.
   • Computational Thinking involves understanding how to "communicate" between the high-level thought processes of humans and the low-level instructions required by computers. Briefing Document: Understanding and Utilizing Classes in Programming
This document synthesizes information from the provided sources, "[#컴퓨테이셔널디자인 06] 쉽게 이해하는 클래스(Class)" and "클래스: 프로그래밍의 핵심 개념과 활용," to provide a comprehensive understanding of the 'Class' concept in programming.

1. Fundamental Components of Programming: Data and Algorithms (Functions)
   At its core, programming involves "utilizing some computer tool to describe and solve problems." This process relies on two primary elements:
   • Data: This refers to the information that needs to be processed. For instance, in the example of a person, data includes their "name, height, weight."
   • Algorithms (Functions/Actions/Behaviors): These are the procedures or functionalities that process and manipulate data. An example given is the 'eat' function, which, when applied to a person's data, might "increase their weight or height."
   The interaction between these two elements is crucial. The sources illustrate this with the scenario of applying the 'eat' function to 'person' data, resulting in "increased height and weight."
2. Overcoming Inefficiency: The Emergence of Lists
   While handling data and actions for a single entity (e.g., one person) is straightforward, managing a large number of entities (e.g., 100 or 1,000 students) individually becomes "cumbersome and inefficient." To address this, concepts like 'List' or 'Array' were introduced.
   • "Instead of a single variable, you create a list of 100 variables."
   • By using a list, multiple data points can be contained within a single variable, allowing for efficient, batch processing by a function. This resolves the inefficiency of applying the same function to individual data points repeatedly.
3. The Genesis of Classes: Unifying Data and Algorithms
   Even with the efficiency gained from lists, a new challenge arose: "Can't we bind such data and algorithms together?" This question led to the development of 'Class'. The core idea behind a class is to logically group data and the functions that operate on that data into a single, cohesive unit.
   • Essence of a Class: A class "binds together" data (variables) and functions (methods).
   • As explained, "What data would be put inside a class? As I mentioned before, data and functions are handled, so data like name, height, weight, address, all these things are put together in one place. And then what functions would there be?    • An 'Object' (or 'Instance') created from a class encapsulates both its defined variables and functions, enabling "easy modification and easy utilization."
4. The Significance of Class Understanding: Paradigms and Practicality
   Understanding the concept of classes is paramount for modern programming for several reasons:
   • Programming Paradigms: Classes are the cornerstone of Object-Oriented Programming (OOP).    • Managing Complex Systems: For intricate systems like CAD platforms or geometry applications, not using classes would result in "extreme complexity and difficulty in maintenance." Classes significantly simplify "maintenance" of such systems.

   • "Understanding what functions a class has... allows for the development of intuitions, like 'this is a basic concept, so I can expand it this way'." This fosters crucial "intuition" for programmers.
5. The Importance of Appropriate Application
   Despite their power and utility, classes are not a panacea. It's crucial to understand that applying classes to every problem is not always the best approach. The source provides an analogy: "It's like taking an airplane to go to a supermarket right in front of you." This highlights that using classes inappropriately can be "irrational." Therefore, discerning when and how to "appropriately" utilize classes, based on the specific problem's characteristics, is a vital skill.

In conclusion, classes are a fundamental concept in programming that allows for the logical encapsulation of data and the functions that operate on it. This enables the efficient design and management of complex systems, enhancing program scalability and maintainability. A thorough understanding of classes cultivates deeper programming knowledge and intuition.

   • 이를 통해 하나의 변수(리스트)에 다수의 데이터를 담고, 이 데이터를 일괄적으로 함수에 적용하여 효율적인 처리가 가능해집니다.
3. 데이터와 알고리즘의 결합: '클래스(Class)'의 필요성
   리스트를 통해 다수의 데이터를 효율적으로 처리할 수 있게 되었지만, 또 다른 고민이 생깁니다. "이런 자료와 이런 자료를 프로세스하는 알고리즘... 이런 알고리즘은 같이 묶을 수 없을까?" 즉, 데이터와 그 데이터를 다루는 함수를 하나의 논리적인 단위로 묶을 필요성이 대두됩니다. 이 지점에서 '클래스' 개념이 등장합니다.
   • 클래스의 본질: 데이터(변수)와 함수(메서드)를 "하나로 묶어 버리는 것"입니다.
   • "클래스 안에 넣어서 어떤 데이터가 들어갈까요? 아깐 즉 데이터랑 일단 펑션 담당하고 했으니까 데이타는 이름 키 몸무게 주소 마 이런 것들 다 하나의 놓는 거죠. 한쪽 기타 집어 넣고 그다음에 성 션은 뭐가 있을까요? 고기를 먹다, 아노 컸다, 마다 잠자다 그래서 이걸 다 묶어 버리는 거 하나 하나로 묶어서 자 너희들은 이제 하나의 사람 이라는 하나의 그냥 객체 하나의 오브젝트야."
   • 클래스를 통해 생성된 '객체(Object)'는 해당 클래스에 정의된 변수와 함수를 모두 포함하며, 이를 통해 "손쉽게 수정하고 손쉽게 활용"할 수 있게 됩니다.
4. 클래스 이해의 중요성: 프로그래밍 패러다임과 실용성
   클래스 개념을 이해하는 것은 현대 프로그래밍 학습에 있어 매우 중요합니다.
   • 프로그래밍 패러다임: 클래스는 객체 지향 프로그래밍(OOP)의 핵심 개념입니다. 다양한 프로그래밍 패러다임(절차 지향, 함수형 등)이 특정 문제 해결에 "더 유리하게끔 혹은 특정 문제를 풀기에 훨씬 더 구체적으로 실수를 방지하고 또 합리적으로 짤 수 있게 했던" 이유로 발전해왔습니다.
   • 복잡한 시스템 관리: 특히 CAD 시스템, 지오메트리 플랫폼 등 복잡하고 상호 연결된 데이터 구조를 다루는 경우, 클래스를 사용하지 않으면 "굉장히 복잡해지고 맨 캔 아이가 너무 힘들어요." 클래스를 통해 시스템의 "유지보수(maintain)"가 훨씬 용이해집니다.
   • 메모리 및 확장성 이해: 클래스 개념을 명확하게 이해하면 메모리 할당(static, instance)과 같은 심화적인 부분뿐만 아니라, Python, C#, Java 등 현대 프로그래밍 언어에서 기본적으로 제공하는 '문자열(String)'과 같은 단순한 자료형조차도 사실은 클래스라는 것을 인지하고 그 내부에 어떤 함수들이 있는지를 이해하는 데 도움이 됩니다.
   • "클래스 어떤 홍천 들이 있는지를 이해하고 이런 부분에도 아 이게 2개 내미니 까이고 새끼 확장시키며 되겠구나 라는 그럼 지도 들 그런 어떤 인튜이션 들이 생겨요." 이는 곧 프로그래밍적인 "직관(intuition)"을 형성하는 데 기여합니다.
5. 적절한 활용의 중요성
   클래스 개념이 매우 강력하고 유용하지만, 모든 상황에 클래스를 적용하는 것이 항상 최선은 아닙니다. "그렇다고 해서 모든 것들 다 클래스로 만든다 이런 것도 사실은 아까 말씀드린 것처럼 맞는 분야에 됐기 때문에 그럴 땐 또 오히려 제가 뭐죠 앞에 슈퍼마켓 하는데 비행기 타고 밝힐 없는 거잖아요." 이는 비합리적인 접근 방식이 될 수 있으므로, 해결하고자 하는 문제의 특성에 맞춰 클래스 개념을 "적절하게" 활용하는 판단력이 필요합니다.

결론적으로, 클래스는 데이터와 그 데이터를 조작하는 함수를 하나의 논리적인 단위로 묶어 복잡한 시스템을 효율적으로 설계하고 관리하며, 프로그램의 확장성과 유지보수성을 높이는 데 필수적인 개념입니다. 이를 통해 프로그래밍에 대한 깊은 이해와 직관을 기를 수 있습니다.
